What does a healthy network look like, and when would I notice?

Radio Bandwidth only calls something out when you would actually hear or feel it. The first columns are what a healthy network measures; the last ones are where people start to notice. A wired link never shows its full label speed: about 6% goes to Ethernet, IP and TCP headers.

What you notice: speed shows up in file copies; delay makes remote control feel laggy and upsets CW and push-to-talk timing; jitter and loss are heard as clicks, stutter and dropouts in live audio; busy delay makes everything lag while something big is transferring.

Common culprits: a cable that only connects at 100 Mb/s (damaged, or only 4 of 8 wires), a cheap or overheating switch, Wi-Fi in the path when you thought it was wired, a busy network during the test, or a VPN relaying traffic through a far away server.
